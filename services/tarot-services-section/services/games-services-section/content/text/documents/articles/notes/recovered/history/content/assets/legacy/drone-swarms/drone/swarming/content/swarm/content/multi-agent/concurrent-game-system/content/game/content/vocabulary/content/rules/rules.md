Rules are the constraints that define what actions are permitted, prohibited, or required within a game.
They create the space of meaningful choice by limiting what [players](./player.md) can do, which is what makes a game a game rather than free [play](./play.md).

Rules need not be written.
Children's games often operate on negotiated consensus; folk games transmit rules orally across generations; and many social games rely on implicit norms that only become visible when someone violates them.
What matters is not the format but the function: rules set up shared expectations about how the game works.

A useful distinction is between *constitutive rules* (which define what the game is — chess without the movement rules is not chess) and *regulatory rules* (which govern conduct — tournament etiquette, time limits, sportsmanship norms).
Constitutive rules determine the [mechanics](./mechanic.md); regulatory rules determine the social context.
[House rules](./house-rule.md) sit in between, modifying constitutive rules by local agreement.
