A house rule is a change to a game's published or traditional [rules](./rules.md) adopted by a particular group of [players](./player.md).
House rules are local: they apply at this table, in this group, for these sessions.
They don't change the game in general; they change the game *as played here*.

House rules emerge for many reasons.
A rule may feel unfair, slow, confusing, or poorly suited to the group's preferred style.
A missing rule may need to be invented (what happens when two players tie?).
A group may want to adjust difficulty, remove content that makes someone uncomfortable, or adapt a game to available materials.
In [role-playing games](../topics/role-playing-games/index.md), house rules are pervasive — most groups modify their [system](../topics/role-playing-games/terms/system.md) to some degree.
In [card games](../topics/card-games/index.md), regional variants often began as house rules that spread.

Structurally, house rules sit between the game-as-designed and the game-as-played.
They reveal what players actually value — when someone changes a rule, they are making a claim about what the game should feel like.
Studying house rules across groups can surface design problems that formal playtesting misses.
