A **scrying medium** is a physical surface or substance used as a focus for [scrying](scrying.md) — a divinatory practice in which the practitioner gazes into the medium to receive images, impressions, or insights.

Common scrying media include:
- **Water** — still water in a dark bowl is one of the oldest and most widely documented media, used in ancient Egyptian, Greek, and many Indigenous traditions
- **Mirrors** — especially dark or obsidian mirrors; the Aztec *tezcatl* (obsidian mirror) and the Elizabethan "shew-stone" tradition both use reflective darkness
- **Crystal** — polished crystal spheres (the classic "crystal ball") use internal reflections and optical distortions to create visual ambiguity
- **Fire and smoke** — fire-gazing (pyromancy) and smoke-reading (capnomancy) use the shifting patterns of flame and smoke as the interpretive substrate
- **Ink or oil on water** — drops of dark liquid on a water surface create temporary patterns that are read as [signs](../../../../../../science/domains/linguistics/domains/semiotics/terms/sign.md)

What makes a surface suitable for scrying is not any mystical [property](../../../../philosophy/terms/property.md) of the material but its capacity to present the viewer with visual ambiguity — enough light and pattern to engage perception, but not enough structure to resolve into a clear, stable image. The medium sits at the threshold between seeing and imagining, and the practitioner's interpretive attention does the rest.
